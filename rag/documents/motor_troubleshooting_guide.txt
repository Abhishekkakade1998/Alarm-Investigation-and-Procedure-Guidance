MOTOR TROUBLESHOOTING AND MAINTENANCE GUIDE
ABB Electric Motors - Technical Reference

1. MOTOR TRIP ALARM INVESTIGATION

Motor trip alarms are protection signals indicating abnormal motor conditions.

1.1 COMMON CAUSES
- Pump cavitation due to low suction pressure
- Mechanical blockage in pump or downstream line
- Bearing failure or excessive friction
- Electrical fault or phase imbalance
- Overload from high system pressure
- Thermal overload from prolonged high current

1.2 DIAGNOSTIC PROCEDURE
Step 1: Check Motor Temperature
- Use infrared thermometer on motor frame
- Normal: 60-80°C in steady state
- Action if > 90°C: Wait for cool-down before restart

Step 2: Verify Motor Insulation
- Measure resistance between windings and frame ground
- Acceptable: > 10 MΩ
- Action if < 5 MΩ: Do not operate, contact maintenance

Step 3: Check Bearing Temperature
- Touch motor bearing housing carefully
- Normal: 60-75°C
- Action if > 80°C: Stop immediately, investigate bearing

Step 4: Listen for Mechanical Sounds
- Squealing: Bearing wear (schedule replacement)
- Grinding: Pump cavitation (check suction pressure)
- Clunking: Loose components (mechanical inspection needed)

1.3 RESTART PROCEDURE
1. Wait minimum 5 minutes after trip
2. Verify suction pressure is above 2 bar
3. Verify discharge back pressure is stable
4. Attempt restart with reduced speed if available
5. If trip repeats within 30 minutes, investigate root cause before further restart attempts

2. THERMAL MANAGEMENT
Motors generate heat during operation due to resistive losses.

2.1 COOLING REQUIREMENTS
- Ambient air circulation required around motor frame
- Minimum 0.5 m³/min air flow
- Avoid dust accumulation on cooling fins
- Keep motor area clean and unobstructed

2.2 BEARING LUBRICATION
- High-temperature grease required for thermal loads
- Bearing life: 40,000-50,000 operating hours
- Quarterly inspection for wear
- Annual regreasing recommended
- Signs of wear: increased noise, temperature rise, vibration

3. VIBRATION ANALYSIS
Excessive vibration indicates mechanical degradation.

3.1 VIBRATION LIMITS
- Acceptable: < 4.0 mm/s RMS
- Warning: 4.0-5.5 mm/s (schedule maintenance)
- Alarm: > 5.5 mm/s (reduce load, prepare shutdown)

3.2 VIBRATION CAUSES
- Cavitation (pump): Typically horizontal axis
- Bearing wear: Radial direction, increases gradually
- Misalignment: Axial vibration component
- Looseness: Multiple frequency components
- Imbalance: Vertical direction, constant

3.3 VIBRATION MITIGATION
- Reduce pump speed by 10-15%
- Increase suction pressure if possible
- Check foundation bolts are tight
- Verify pump-motor coupling alignment
- Schedule precision alignment if misalignment suspected

4. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE

DAILY
- Visual inspection for leaks or damage
- Listen for unusual sounds
- Check motor frame temperature with touch
- Record vibration level if instrumented

WEEKLY
- Measure bearing temperature at 4 points
- Check air circulation around motor
- Inspect for dust or debris accumulation

MONTHLY
- Measure insulation resistance (megohm check)
- Verify cooling water flow if water-cooled
- Check for any visible cracking or damage
- Review operational hours counter

QUARTERLY
- Perform detailed vibration spectrum analysis
- Inspect motor mounting bolts
- Check bearing lubrication
- Measure winding temperature

ANNUALLY
- Professional motor service inspection
- Bearing replacement if > 40,000 hours
- Insulation system testing
- Shaft alignment check
- Thermal camera survey

5. ALARM RESPONSE PROCEDURES

Motor Overload Alarm (Thermal Protection)
- Indicates motor current drawing 110-125% of rated current
- Cause: High pump discharge pressure, cavitation, or mechanical resistance
- Action: Reduce pump speed, verify system pressure, check suction
- If alarm clears within 2 minutes: Monitor closely
- If alarm persists: Reduce load further or initiate shutdown

Motor Phase Imbalance Alarm
- Occurs when three-phase motor receives unbalanced current
- Cause: Electrical supply issue or motor winding fault
- Action: Contact electrical maintenance immediately
- Do not continue operation if imbalance > 5%

Motor Bearing Temperature Alarm
- Triggered when bearing temperature > 85°C
- Common cause: Pump cavitation, high vibration
- Action: Reduce pump speed immediately
- Investigate suction pressure and vibration
- If temperature does not drop within 10 minutes: Shutdown

6. MOTOR SELECTION FOR CRITICAL APPLICATIONS
When selecting replacement motors for high-reliability systems:
- Ensure motor is sized for actual pump duty (not just nominal)
- Specify high-efficiency motor (IE3 or better)
- Consider thermal class F for thermal margin
- Request thermal protection with adjustable setpoint
- Verify bearing design is appropriate for vibration environment
- Check alignment and coupling quality

7. MAINTENANCE LOG REQUIREMENTS
Record for every motor service event:
- Date and time
- Service performed (inspection, lubrication, repair)
- Motor temperature before and after
- Bearing condition assessment
- Any replacement parts used
- Operator and technician names
- Next scheduled service date
- Any recommendations noted
